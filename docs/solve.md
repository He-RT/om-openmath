# Equation solving

The solver is specified in [PLAN.md §8](plan/PLAN.md#8-算法规格) and the acceptance corpus in §14. Polynomial candidate kernels are implemented; the user-facing Solve builtin and complete dispatcher are subsequent M9/M10 tasks.

P0/P1 preparation is implemented as `om_solve::normalize::normalize`. It preserves original denominator/function exclusions and raw residuals, separates logical branches, infers variable domains and prepares uncanceled polynomial numerators. The public Solve algorithms and frontend integration remain in subsequent M9/M10 tasks.

When variables are inferred and there are more variables than equality constraints, OpenMath emits `Solve::svars` and solves for the first n variables in name order; remaining symbols are parameters (PLAN §7.5). Constraint-only input retains its inferred variables. Explicit variable lists preserve their requested order. These are OpenMath compatibility choices. Multivariate inequalities and logical expansion beyond 64 branches are marked unsupported rather than reported as an empty solution.

Chained `Unequal[a,b,c]` produces all pairwise nonzero restrictions; `Element[{x,y}, Reals]` and `Element[x|y, Reals]` apply the domain to each variable. These follow the official [Unequal](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/Unequal.html) and [Element](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/Element.html) documentation.

The M9.3 candidate kernel `om_solve::univariate::poly_uni` solves exact rational polynomials whose complete factorization contains only linear and quadratic factors. It clears rational denominators, uses Yun and certified integer factorization, composes root multiplicities, and orders rational-coefficient roots by certified real/imaginary algebraic comparisons. It also extracts independent coefficient content and zero-root powers for parameter polynomials before applying linear/quadratic formulas. Later tasks extend the same kernel below. All candidates carry construction provenance; original restrictions and complete verification remain dispatcher stages.

Parameter roots retain generic nonzero-leading and coefficient-denominator assumptions. The default keeps these in computation metadata; `MaxExtra::All` exposes them through `ConditionalExpression`, and `Count(n)` attaches a complete conjunction only when it fits. Wolfram-shaped output repeats each root according to its multiplicity, as specified for OpenMath. An unsupported component makes the whole candidate set unevaluated. Integer/rational domain filters are described under M9.12 below; this does not yet expose a user-facing Solve builtin.

M9.4 adds `a*x^n+b` radicals, maximal `p(x^k)` substitution, and even palindromic polynomials via `z=x+1/x`. Every inverse branch is retained and its multiplicity is multiplied by that of the reduced root. The reduced equation must have wholly radical roots; since M9.5, a reduction that only yields Root objects falls back to Root objects of the original factor. Auxiliary symbols avoid existing coefficients and compound expression heads. Symbolic coefficient content and zero-root powers can be combined with these reductions.

For Q factors above degree two, formula roots are matched uniquely to certified algebraic roots of the original factor. Real-first ordering uses disjoint directed enclosures for strict comparisons and exact conjugate/projection identities for overlapping components. Algebraic certification currently inherits the degree-64 and 16,352-bit limits of om-poly. M9.5 falls back atomically to the original factor's Root objects when radical association fails; unavailable certification or ordering returns `Unsupported`. Native Root function/polynomial/index identity uses the certified batch directly. Parameter results use canonical symbolic ordering.

The checked `om_poly::algebraic_roots` batch constructor shares the original root-numbering contract with `algebraic_root`, returning all distinct certified values from one factorization and isolation. This solver uses that batch and reuses the certified complex disks. Six release candidate-kernel probes spanning cubic/quintic binomials, nested substitutions and mixed quartics take 15–109 ms on the development Mac; these timings do not yet cover the future complete Solve dispatcher.

M9.5 supplies general `Root[Function[polynomial(Slot[1])], k]` candidates with one-based indices after factorization and special reductions. `Cubics -> False` and `Quartics -> False` remain the defaults for general factors. Enabling them uses depressed Cardano and Ferrari formulas; Ferrari independently enables Cardano for its resolvent, chooses a certified positive real resolvent root, and uses a biquadratic reduction when the depressed linear coefficient is zero. Generic parameter coefficients keep symbolic Root objects and their leading assumptions. Multiplicities still come from Yun and complete integer factorization.

Formula-derived realness is remembered only after certified root association. Ball evaluation retains the formula's computed real component and intersects with the proven real axis, including rational combinations of flattened sums. Missing certificates use bounded algebraic proof with recursion protection; failed proof can decline numeric evaluation. Tiny imaginary intervals alone never certify realness. Recorded steps contain the actual substitutions, discriminants, eliminant, factors and formula results; `record_steps=false` suppresses all recording even with an enabled sink.

M9.6 honors `SolveOptions::domain = Reals` at the polynomial candidate boundary. Distinct real-root counts come from exact isolation of the Yun square-free parts, preserving multiplicities in repeated inputs. A native rational Root is real exactly when its index does not exceed its defining factor's real count; that count is shared across the factor's Root objects. Radical candidates use directed imaginary enclosures at 64, 256, 1024 and 4096 bits. An interval excluding zero proves nonreal, an exact zero imaginary ball proves real, and exactly the certified number of remaining Q roots proves them real by completeness. A small radius or zero midpoint alone is insufficient.

Unresolved realness is retained with `Solve::real` diagnostics in `PolynomialRoots::messages` and actual Note steps. DomainFilter records retained/rejected distinct candidate counts and before/after values. Disabling steps preserves diagnostics. Parameter assumptions and root expressions stay attached to retained solutions; numeric refinement can reject tiny nonzero imaginary perturbations without claiming realness for an unresolved interval.

M9.7 adds `om_solve::univariate::radical_path` for rational-base fractional powers, including common fractional bases, negative powers and nested radicals. Up to two suitable radicals use at most four isolation/power steps; the general path introduces axes inside out and eliminates them in reverse order with exact resultants. Variable-dependent opaque functions are rejected rather than treated as independent coefficients. An identically zero eliminant cannot establish a continuous principal-branch solution set.

Every radical candidate is checked against the original expression and its original exclusions, including denominator holes erased by canonical cancellation. `VerifyMode::Never` cannot disable these checks. Exact zero yields `Exact`; an inconclusive residual must pass both 128- and 512-bit zero-containing ball checks with radii below `2^-(bits-16)` to yield `Numeric { digits: 33 }`. Other candidates produce `DropExtraneous`, with diagnostics for failed inconclusive verification. Parameter conditions remain available in metadata and through `MaxExtra`; caller interruption propagates through bounded optional exact checks. Radical elimination yields distinct assignments with multiplicity one, while direct polynomial delegation preserves Yun multiplicities. Recorded isolation, powers, resultants and verification contain the actual expressions; disabled recording suppresses every event.

M9.8 adds `om_solve::univariate::transcendental_path`. It unifies affine exponential kernels with commensurate slopes, integer bases sharing a primitive perfect-power base, and sine/cosine with a common argument. The transformed algebraic equation uses the existing polynomial solver, then explicit inverses recover every represented periodic branch. Elementary outer integer/fractional powers use complete polynomial inverse relations followed by principal-branch checks. Reals absolute values split only when the argument is proved real; unsupported complex absolute-value arguments remain unevaluated.

Generated integer periods are stored in `Solution::constants`, use the configured parameter head, avoid indices already in the input, and share one index across a kernel's inverse alternatives. Exact period shifts remove duplicate families. Real families with an exactly imaginary period and real offset collapse to the zero-period member; other unresolved families keep `Element[value, Reals]`. Principal Log enforces `-Pi < Im[value] <= Pi`; symbolic inverse-trig solutions retain both strip restrictions and their inverse-composition equality. The plan's uncertain complex ArcTan strip boundaries conservatively remain unevaluated. ProductLog uses its exact defining identity and the specified real branches; the principal Complexes branch emits `Solve::ifun` as required and does not claim a full Lambert-W family or general numeric ProductLog evaluation.

Original residual/pole checks are mandatory for transcendental candidates. Integer families check three permitted members from `0, 1, -1, 2, -2`, retaining conditions for excluded members. Parameter verification uses three fixed-seed rational samples and skips zero generic assumptions or exclusions. Finite candidates are `Exact` or pass the same 128/512-bit residual thresholds as radicals; Numeric verification remains marked inconclusive in structured Verify steps. Unsupported independent kernels or unavailable inverse branches preserve the whole equation as unevaluated. Substitution, inversion, back substitution, verification and real filtering record actual data; disabled recording and inverse-function options are honored. Nested inversions are bounded to 24; dense power inverse degrees/denominators to 4096.

M9.9 adds `om_solve::linear_system` for one normalized affine conjunction. All rows use a shared exact `Z[parameters]` coefficient context after rational denominator clearing. The solver reuses `om_poly::linear_solve_observed`, an observation adapter over the existing Bareiss and exact fraction back substitution; original `linear_solve` and `bareiss` interfaces remain available. Row swaps and completed integral updates produce exact Scale/Add steps, with the final matrix taken from the live kernel and intermediate expressions certifying the equivalent field operations.

Pivot variables appear in requested order, while nonpivot variables keep their original names and are omitted from the rule list. Underdetermined systems emit `Solve::svars`; generic pivot and contradiction conditions remain in metadata and obey `MaxExtra`. Original zero exclusions reject assignments; parameter/free-variable restrictions become conditions. Exact residuals receive `Exact`, and inconclusive residuals retain the exact linear construction's `ByConstruction` provenance. Identities return `All`; a constraint-only domain returns a conditioned empty rule list. Explicit per-variable Reals restrictions are honored, with unresolved symbolic values retaining membership conditions. Display approximations are optional and finite; huge exact values remain exact. Nonlinear systems, independent disjunctions and inequalities route to their later algorithms, and integer/rational domains follow M9.12 below.

M9.10 adds `om_solve::poly_system`. It constructs one rational polynomial context, computes a grevlex Gröbner basis, uses FGLM for finite ideals, and uses a lex basis for positive-dimensional systems. Finite eliminants are split into certified irreducible factors; each component uses exact shape coordinates reduced modulo the defining root polynomial. When shape fails, up to five deterministic fresh primitive linear combinations try to separate the points. Root-valued coordinates preserve their defining Root identity. Every finite component must complete before any result is returned.

Finite multiplicity comes from the original primary quotient's standard-monomial count divided by the conjugate residue-field degree. Powers created during elimination do not establish point multiplicity. This distinguishes the lengths four of `(x^2,y^2)` and three of `(x^2,y^2,xy)`, while preserving distinct and conjugate repeated points. Primary quotient enumeration is capped at 65536 standard monomials; root certification keeps the existing algebraic limits. A zero primitive value evaluates coordinate constants directly, avoiding `0^0`.

Positive-dimensional systems prefer later independent axes as free parameters and solve available linear/quadratic triangular relations. Higher-degree or unavailable relations stay explicit conditions with `Solve::svars`. Generic assumptions remain in metadata and follow `MaxExtra`; requested free Reals axes retain membership conditions, and constrained external coefficient axes retain equalities rather than appearing as requested-variable rules. Exact source residuals yield `Exact`; shape/FGLM or conditioned triangular certificates retain `ByConstruction` for inconclusive zero checks. Original poles and explicit domains are enforced; unresolved numeric exclusions remain wholly unevaluated. Numeric coordinate ordering uses directed exact intervals and algebraic projection comparisons before later variables, so display rounding cannot reverse close points. Actual basis, eliminant, substitution, coordinate recovery and verification steps are recorded, with disabled recording suppressed.

M9.11 adds `om_solve::substitution_system` for mixed conjunctions. It probes existing univariate polynomial/radical/transcendental kernels, selects the smallest `(discrete branch count, expression size)` in stable input/variable order, and substitutes one original variable per level. Only the selected solver records derivation steps. Complete discrete branching is capped at 64; any incomplete branch declines the entire system. Free axes keep their original names, including compound axes, and normalized duplicate requested axes are removed.

Intermediate principal-branch verification is deferred through internal candidate adapters because the other solving variables are provisional parameters. After full back substitution every original residual and pole is checked, using exact proofs or the existing 128/512-bit ball thresholds and permitted period/parameter samples. `VerifyMode::Never` cannot disable this final check; no mixed candidate is returned as Unverified. Independent inverse families receive distinct integer constants beyond all names already in the original input. Generic assumptions obey MaxExtra, and explicit variable domains remain enforced.

If substitution fails, a bounded fallback replaces reversible kernels with fresh axes and adds `s²+c²=1` for same-argument sine/cosine pairs. It solves the resulting polynomial system and recovers all represented inverse branches without recursively lifting again. Incomplete auxiliary assignments decline atomically; auxiliary names cannot appear in final rules. Remaining relations on integer periods or free parameters stay explicit conditions. Unsupported opaque functions and disjunction orchestration remain later stages; integer/rational domains follow M9.12 below.

M9.12 honors Integers/Rationals through the existing univariate, linear, polynomial-system and mixed-system entry points. Closed exact coordinates are reduced to certified algebraic values: rational values satisfy Rationals, and rational values with denominator one satisfy Integers. Certified nonrational algebraics are removed. Floating proximity to an integer is never evidence. Symbolic rules and free axes retain exact membership conditions; unavailable closed certificates decline the entire result with `Solve::dom`. Real filtering precedes discrete filtering for elementary transcendental inversion, so `Exp[x]=1` over Integers yields only zero.

For affine integer conjunctions the solver clears exact rational row denominators and reuses column HNF's unimodular certificate. Every integer solution is the returned particular vector plus the complete integer nullspace basis multiplied by independent generated integer constants. This covers `2x+3y=1`, simultaneous rank-deficient equations and divisibility contradictions. All primary system entry points share this lattice route. Per-variable explicit integer restrictions also select it when all requested axes are integers. Symbolic coefficient HNF remains unsupported; mixed domains can retain explicit membership conditions.

The original uncanceled pole restrictions remain conditions on the lattice or remove a unique pole assignment. New generated constants avoid all indices in the source. Existing source constants retain their guards and are sampled during mandatory original checks. Genuine coordinate recovery, source verification and domain filtering are recorded; disabling steps suppresses them all and interruption propagates. Multiplicities of surviving polynomial roots are unchanged.
